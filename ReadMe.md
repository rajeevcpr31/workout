# 🍽️ Basic Nutrition Explained

Think of your body like a **car**, and food like **fuel + spare parts**.

---

## ⚡ What Are Calories?

Calories are simply **units of energy** in food — "how much fuel is in this tank."

- Everything you eat has a calorie count
- Move more or build muscle → you burn more calories
- Eat more calories than you burn → you gain weight
- Eat less than you burn → you lose weight

Your body uses this energy to:
- Keep your heart and organs working
- Walk and move
- Digest food
- Exercise
- Build and repair muscle

**Examples of calorie-containing foods:** Rice, Roti, Milk, Eggs, Oil — all of these contain calories.

---

## ⚖️ Maintenance, Surplus & Deficit

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Maintenance Calories** | The amount of food energy needed to keep your body weight stable | Body uses 2,400 → you eat 2,400 |
| **Calorie Surplus** | Eating slightly *more* than you burn — supports muscle growth & weight gain | Body uses 2,400 → you eat 2,600 |
| **Calorie Deficit** | Eating *less* than you burn — usually causes weight loss over time | Body uses 2,400 → you eat 2,000 |

> 💡 **Note:** If your goal is to build muscle, a small surplus generally works better than a deficit. (If your goal is fat loss instead, a deficit would be more appropriate — adjust based on *your* specific goal.)

---

## 🧱 Macronutrients ("Macros")

The three big categories of food your body needs in **large quantities**.

### 🥩 Protein — "The Bricks"
Protein is the main building and repair material for muscle. Training breaks muscle down; protein provides the material to rebuild it stronger.

**Good Indian protein sources:**
- Eggs, Milk, Curd, Paneer
- Chicken, Fish
- Soy chunks, Tofu
- Dal, Rajma, Chana, Sprouts
- Greek yogurt / high-protein curd

> ⚠️ Dal + rice together give a useful mix of protein and carbs, but most people still need extra protein sources to hit a muscle-building target.

### 🍚 Carbohydrates — "The Construction Workers"
Your body's primary, clean energy source — fuels workouts and daily brain function.

**Good sources:**
- Rice, Roti, Oats
- Potatoes, Sweet potatoes
- Poha, Upma, Idli, Dosa
- Fruits
- Dal and beans

> ✅ Carbs are **not** automatically fattening. Eating more total calories than you need, over time, is what generally causes weight gain.

### 🧈 Fats — "The Operating System"
A slower-burning fuel. Essential for hormone production (especially testosterone, which drives muscle growth), joint health, and absorbing certain vitamins.

**Good sources:**
- Peanuts, Almonds, Cashews, Seeds
- Ghee, Groundnut oil, Mustard oil, Olive oil
- Eggs, Fish, Milk, Curd, Paneer

> ✅ You do **not** need to eliminate fat to get abs.

---

## 🧹 Fiber — "The Cleanup Crew"

The part of carb-containing food that isn't digested. Helps digestion, bowel regularity, and keeps you feeling full.

**Good sources:**
- Vegetables, Fruits
- Oats, Whole-wheat roti
- Dal, Rajma, Chana, Beans, Seeds

🎯 **Target:** roughly **25–35 g per day**

---

## 🔩 Micronutrients (Vitamins & Minerals — "The Spark Plugs")

Small helper nutrients needed in tiny amounts to run internal biological processes. Mostly covered by a varied diet of fruits, vegetables, and dairy.

| Nutrient | Good Sources |
|---|---|
| **Calcium** | Milk, curd, paneer, ragi |
| **Iron** | Beans, leafy vegetables, meat, eggs |
| **Vitamin C** | Amla, guava, oranges, lemon |
| **Vitamin A** | Carrots, spinach, colorful vegetables |
| **Vitamin B12** | Eggs, milk, dairy, fish, meat |
| **Vitamin D** | Sunlight exposure; supplementation if needed (under medical guidance) |
| **Potassium** | Bananas, potatoes, dal, fruits |

> 🥗 A **varied diet** is more useful than chasing one "superfood."

### 🔑 Key Takeaways
- Calories in vs. calories out determines weight gain/loss
- Protein rebuilds muscle, carbs fuel it, fats support hormones
- Fiber, vitamins, and minerals keep everything running smoothly
- No single food is magic — variety and consistency matter most

## Estimated Calories and Protein Requirements

These are approximate, not exact science — think of them as a smart starting point that we'll adjust based on how your body responds.

| Metric | Estimate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Maintenance calories** | ~2,350–2,400 kcal/day | Based on your age, height, weight, and a lightly active lifestyle plus 4–5 workouts/week |
| **Target calories (to build muscle)** | ~2,600–2,700 kcal/day | A modest surplus (~300 kcal above maintenance) — enough to grow muscle without gaining much fat |
| **Protein target** | ~130 g/day (about 2 g per kg body weight) | This is the beginner "muscle-building" range recommended for someone your size |
| **Fat target** | ~70–75 g/day | Roughly 25% of your calories — needed for hormones |
| **Carb target** | ~370–380 g/day | The rest of your calories — fuels your workouts and recovery |
| **Fiber target** | 30–35 g/day | For digestion and overall health |

### How to adjust over time:

- Weigh yourself weekly (same day, same time, ideally morning, empty stomach)
- Gaining less than ~0.25 kg/week → add ~150–200 more calories/day (e.g., extra roti, more rice, a handful of nuts)
- Gaining more than ~0.5 kg/week → you're likely gaining too much fat; reduce by ~150–200 calories/day
- Not gaining at all after 3 weeks → increase food intake

> 📌 **Note:** Muscle grows slowly — expect roughly 0.25–0.5 kg of bodyweight gain per week as a healthy beginner pace.

These numbers are targets, not strict rules. For example, if you eat 125 g protein instead of 130 g, that is completely fine.

**Why are carbs relatively high?** You are trying to build muscle, you are relatively light, and you will be training several days per week. Carbs help you train harder and recover better.
